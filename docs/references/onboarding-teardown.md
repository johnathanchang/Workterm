# Onboarding teardown: Prof Insider, AwardScholar, Cal AI

Checked on 3 November 2026 (chapter 09: one reference per screen).

- **Prof Insider** (profinsider.com/onboarding) and **AwardScholar** (awardscholar.com/onboarding): walked live in a browser, up to the sign-in wall. They use the same template, so they are likely built by the same team.
- **Cal AI**: the address cal.ai now opens Cal.com, a scheduling app. Cal AI (calai.app) onboards only inside its phone app, so the notes below come from its well-known flow (playbook chapter 09 already names it), not a live walk.

## Prof Insider, screen by screen

| # | Screen | What it does | Why it works |
|---|--------|--------------|--------------|
| 1 | "How did you hear about us?" (8 taps) | Asks first, while the student is still keen | Free ad attribution on day one |
| 2 | "Which of these sounds most like you?" (3 cards) | Curious / too slow / want faster | Splits users by intent. "No wrong answers" lowers the stakes |
| 3 | "You don't need the perfect resume." | An animated Sent folder fills up, then a professor replies. Buttons: Import resume / Do it later | Teaches the core idea (volume wins) before asking for anything |
| 3b | Pop-up: "Emails are better with a resume" | Only when they skip. Add my resume / Skip for now | One soft second ask, never a block |
| 4 | Education level + graduation year (big buttons) | Tap, tap, Continue | No dropdowns. Each question says why it is asked |
| 5 | "Where do you currently study?" Canada / US / Elsewhere, then school search | Two-stage: country first, then type-ahead | Short list first, so the search is easier |
| 6 | "What do you want to research?" 13 fields, pick 1 to 3 | Broad chips | Quick wins with taps |
| 7 | "Anything more specific?" topics for the picked field | Skippable | Depth only for those who want it |
| 8 | "Where do you want to do research?" Local / Remote / Either | Each with a one-line explainer | Plain choices |
| 9 | "What kind of position?" multi-select with sub-lines | Summer / school year / thesis / grad | Shapes the ask in the email |
| 10 | "Ready to meet your matches?" Find → Match → Email ticks off with live counts, plus a scrolling wall of student posts | Build-up before the reveal | Social proof right before the wall |
| 11 | "Sign in to see your matches" with a Gmail compose mock | "Signing in doesn't give us access to your Gmail." | Answers the biggest fear in one line |

Every screen has: Back at the top left, a thin progress bar, and a "~2 min" time left that drops to "~1 min" near the end. The Continue button stays faded until the screen is answered.

## AwardScholar: what differs

- Opens with a **hook screen** before any question: "Most scholarship money goes unclaimed", a big dollar figure and one button, "See what I qualify for". Also has "I already have an account".
- Same motivation question, same country question (with flags), then dropdowns for level, grade and year. Dropdowns feel slower than Prof Insider's big buttons.
- "~3 min" timer, blue theme. Otherwise the same template.

## Cal AI (from its known flow)

- 20+ one-tap questions, one per screen, big pill buttons, progress bar.
- **Value screens between questions**: a chart showing "Cal AI vs a normal diet", and "losing X is a realistic target" encouragement that uses the user's own answers.
- "Thank you for trusting us" with a privacy promise, right before the personal part.
- **"Setting everything up for you"**: a percent counter and checklist, then the custom plan reveal (the aha moment).
- Paywall with a **trial timeline**: Today (full access), a reminder before it ends, then billing. Honest and clear.

## Patterns to copy

1. One question per screen. Tap a card and move on, no typing where a tap will do.
2. Thin progress bar plus "~2 min left" at the top. Back at the top left.
3. A hook screen with one promise and one button.
4. "Which sounds most like you?" and "How did you hear about us?" These are cheap to ask and give us go-to-market data.
5. A line under each question saying why we ask.
6. A value screen before the resume ask, and a soft pop-up when they skip.
7. A build-up screen with animated steps before the reveal.
8. Sign-in that says what we cannot see in their Gmail.
9. A paywall with a trial timeline.

## What we do differently, on purpose

- **Matches before sign-up.** Both reference sites hide the matches behind sign-in. Our rule 10 and the brief show matches first. That is the aha moment, and it is our edge.
- **No made-up numbers or fake reviews.** They show "107,003 professors" and walls of student posts. We are pre-launch, so we show only true statements. Real proof can go in once we have students (chapter 09, rule 8: honest).
- **No placement promise** anywhere in the copy.
